# Communication Principles for Project Managers

## 1. Know your audience
| Audience | Cares about | Give them |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Executives / sponsor | Outcomes, cost, risk, decisions | One page, decisions first |
| Managers | Impact on their teams and dates | Timelines, what they need to do |
| Delivery team | Tasks, blockers, priorities | Detail, clear ownership |
| End users | What changes for them, and when | Short, practical, friendly |
| Vendors | Scope, dates, defects | Precise, written, tracked |

## 2. Lead with the bottom line
Start every update with the answer: *"We are on track for go-live on 14 March. One decision is needed by Friday."* Put the detail underneath for anyone who wants it.

## 3. Use RAG status honestly
- 🟢 **Green:** on track, no help needed.
- 🟡 **Amber:** at risk; we have a plan, but watch it.
- 🔴 **Red:** off track; a decision or help is needed now.

An honest Amber this week is better than a surprise Red next month.

## 4. No surprises
Bad news travels best early. Raise a risk when it is still cheap to fix, and always bring an option or recommendation with the problem.

## 5. Write it down
Verbal agreements fade. Confirm decisions in writing (minutes, an email or the decision log) within 24 hours.

## 6. Make actions unmissable
Every action needs **what, who and by when**. "We'll look into it" is not an action.

## 7. Choose the right channel
| Situation | Best channel |
|-----------|--------------|
| Routine progress | Written status report |
| Decision needed | Meeting + written pack |
| Urgent issue | Call first, then confirm in writing |
| Wide announcement | Email / intranet / Teams post |
| Sensitive feedback | One-to-one conversation |

## 8. Close the loop
Tell people what happened to their feedback. It builds trust and keeps them engaged.
